# Stage-by-stage flow

Each step is: parent packs → child works → Gate Runner captures and seals → Coordinator requests the gate (with an attempt id) → Gate Runner runs it → Coordinator advances only on a PASS for that attempt. A failed gate goes back to the owning stage. Operational roles (repair round 1, 2026-11-22): the **Gate Runner** runs every gate and the capture, promote, seal and close tools; the **Database Connector** executes the SQL Analyst's SELECT-only SQL read-only on the frozen snapshot and reports the snapshot it opened; the **Coordinator** requests each gate run with an attempt id and advances a stage only on a PASS for the attempt it requested. The parent orchestrates and never does those three jobs itself.

## Stage 1 and Stage 2 conversation (live Stakeholder, owner-approved 2026-11-16)

Stages 1 and 2 assume the work is requested by a real person, back and forth. The Stakeholder (a child role, docs/roles/stakeholder.md) plays the business person in a group chat with the owner and the parent. Child-bot packets and reviews stay one to one; only the Stakeholder conversation happens in the group chat.

1. **Persona.** The parent writes a one-paragraph persona from the public brief (job title, the decision owned, what matters). The Stakeholder reads only the brief and the persona.
2. **Stakeholder opens.** The Stakeholder opens the case in its own words: the decision, any deadline, the data it hands over.
3. **Clarifying questions.** The parent asks clarifying questions. The Stakeholder answers from the brief, says "the brief doesn't say" when it is silent, and labels any business preference as a preference.
4. **The owner weighs in.** The owner may comment, decide a point, or add a twist (a changed deadline, a new question, a changed rule). Only the owner can change the brief's decision rule, thresholds or definitions.
5. **Capture.** Every Stakeholder and owner message the stage relies on is captured exactly by the Gate Runner, with its timestamp, by `python3 -m gates.stakeholder capture` into `captures/stage1_chat/` (Stage 2: `captures/stage2_chat/`): one write-once file per message, chmod a-w, its sha256 computed from the file and `SHA256SUMS` regenerated from the files. No hash is typed by hand. The parent registers each statement it relies on (`gates.stakeholder statement`: capture id, exact quote, kind `brief_fact`, `preference`, `clarification`, `twist` or `rule_change`).
6. **Write-up.** The Lead Designer writes the Stage 1 decision document: the agreed decision and the agreed question in plain words (`agreed_question`), with `sources` that cite brief quotes and the registered statements. The Second Designer and Risk Reviewer review it with the captures as inputs.
7. **Owner lock.** The owner locks the agreed decision and question in his own words in the chat. The Gate Runner captures that message and binds it to the exact decision-document hash (`gates.stakeholder lock`, `artifacts/stage1_owner_lock.json`). The Stakeholder's "looks good" is business feedback and never a lock.
8. **Stage 2 (framing).** The Lead Designer drafts the precise framing from the brief and the Stage 1 captures. The parent shares it in the group chat; the Stakeholder reacts as a business reader and the owner weighs in, captured into the Stage 2 record. The framing's `sources` cite brief quotes and Stage 1 or Stage 2 statements, its threshold traces to the brief or an owner-authorised change, and the owner locks the exact framing hash in his own words (`artifacts/stage2_owner_lock.json`). The Second Designer critique and Risk Reviewer framing check are unchanged.

G1 and G2 check all of this (docs/gates.md). The R receipt fields `stakeholder_confirmation` (Stage 1) and `stakeholder_approval` (Stage 2) record the Stakeholder's business sign-off only; `human_analyst_approval` is backed by the captured owner lock.

**Freeze counts restart at zero.** The live Stakeholder changes the Stage 1 and Stage 2 inputs, so every stage's clean-in-a-row count toward the workflow freeze restarts at zero with the first case run this way (planned: case03). Counts from earlier cases do not carry over.

## Notes
- **Before any stop on a missing input.** A child that finds an input or mapping missing first checks the public schema, brief and bundle files in its packet. If they settle it, it writes `DERIVED: <item> from <path>: <how>` and keeps working. Only if they do not does it stop with `MISSING INPUT: <item>`. The parent re-checks the public files and records the result in `artifacts/derivability.json`; the Gate Runner runs GD (G0 runs the same checks): a derivable item goes back to the child as a new `-vN` packet with the derivation; only a non-derivable item is escalated to the owner. No stage closes as "unresolved" on a derivable item.
- Descriptive rates and differences are part of the expected Stage 4 and 5 results when the question compares groups. Label them descriptive or associational, never causal (G3, G5).
- Every packet passes G0 (packet integrity) before it is sent, and every reply is checked by G0 when captured. A child's work counts only when its reply ends with `ANSWERED <PACKET_ID>` after a non-blank list of produced files.
- After a repair of a reviewed file (for example a Stage 2 framing repair), the current version goes back to the required reviewers before the gate. G2, G3 and G5 fail a review of a superseded file.
- The first Stage 2 framing states the capacity rule (including whether an option may be taken at a loss) and the decision unit (pooled or per site/segment) explicitly.
- Stage 5 order: Lead Designer write-up and claims ledger → G5P → Second Designer inference review and Risk Reviewer number audit → owner finish approval of the exact package hashes → R finish receipt → RP `complete finish` → G5 → `finalize` → G6 certificate. In short (the same wording in the gate README, docs/gates.md and the parent role): owner approves the exact finish package -> R `complete finish` -> G5 -> R `finalize` -> G6. The R finish receipt needs `human_analyst_approval` APPROVED and G5 reads that receipt, so the owner finish approval comes before G5; the owner stop still comes before `finalize`. G4d never overwrites a Workflow Gate report the R Procedure Gate has recorded.
- **Stage 3 lock completeness.** The locked design carries every field the R Workflow Gate reads at G4d, and the Grok design declares `decision_thresholds` (id, number, statement; `value` is accepted as the same field) and any `owner_holds` (owner source, and a decision output that records the rule's action and the hold; shape in docs/roles/lead-designer.md). G3 checks all of them.
- **Next stage's entry checks at lock.** A design is not locked until the next stage's entry gates pass on it. G3 runs the G4a/G4b shape checks on the locked fixtures (each worked answer compared with itself with the fixture's keys and tolerance, every tolerance in the `{fields, absolute_tolerance, relative_tolerance}` shape) and checks that every versioned docs/stage3 file the locked design names carries the locked design version.
- **Closing a stage.** The Gate Runner closes and seals a stage only after `python3 -m gates.close <closing gate> <root>` exits 0 (G1, G2, G2F, G3, G4d, G5 then G6). Every tool step that runs a gate exits non-zero on FAIL.
- **Captured replies.** A saved reply is recorded as `captured`; only `python3 -m gates.packet promote <root> <id>` makes it `answered`, and only when G0 passes. A captured packet blocks the close and G6.
- **Stage 5 companions, status words and ids.** A companion file of the finish package is declared (`companions` in the ledger or approval, or `companion <path> sha256 <hash>` in the approval text) and goes to both Stage 5 reviewers. G5P/G5 fail a report or companion that calls an open G4c finding resolved or closed, or that does not carry a finding the Stage 4 triage says is carried. Stage 5 review ids carry the role (SD5-, RR5-, X5-) and `uncovered_claims` is `[]` or claim ids. A JSON a reviewer produced that cites the ledger at an old hash needs an H1 historical-send record (G5P/G5 check it).
- **Stage 4 extract and corrections.** The SQL Analyst writes the extract; the parent never writes it. A builder that must correct its own file before send logs it in `artifacts/stage4/corrections.json` (old and new hash, role, reason, time); the Risk Reviewer reviews that log at G4c. No unlock and re-freeze.
- **Stage 5 precheck.** Each Stage 5 ledger is prechecked with G5P before it goes to review and again before placement; the latest precheck log goes into every Stage 5 review packet. A revision brief that changes a report path asks for each claim's `deliverable` to be updated.
- **Stage 5 report content.** Every decision threshold appears as a number with its design id. A triggered owner hold is reported beside the action the rule decides (rule action + hold id on one line), and is put to the owner; the hold is never dropped and never replaces the rule's action. Disclosures say what the record says, and a corrected disclosure is swept through every ledger field that repeats it.
- Stages 1–2 never see data values. The Stakeholder does not discuss data values in the chat, even when the brief carries an inline dataset. Feasibility sees structure and quality only, not values or the answer key, and cannot add or reshape questions.
- Stage 1 reviews (owner-approved Grok mapping): the Lead Designer writes the Stage 1 decision document from the brief and the Stage 1 captures; the Second Designer rebuilds it blind from the same inputs (brief, persona, conversation record and captures) and then compares the two; the Risk Reviewer red-teams it, including whether each cited statement says what the document claims. The parent pins the workflow; the Gate Runner runs the start gate G1. Stage 1 packets cite this mapping by name ("Stage 1 reviews, docs/stages.md").
- At Stage 2, the Second Designer critiques the Lead Designer's framing and the Risk Reviewer checks that framing alongside it. The Risk Reviewer also reviews G2F feasibility.
- At Stage 3, the Second Designer first writes its own independent design from the Stage 3 inputs, without seeing the Lead Designer's draft or reasoning. After that design is sealed, the parent sends the lead's design so the Second Designer can compare and report differences. The Risk Reviewer red-teams the design.
- Also at Stage 3, the SQL Analyst writes the SQL source gates (G3S: extraction completeness, keys, joins, counts) against the design; the Gate Runner runs them through the Database Connector; the Risk Reviewer reviews them. G3K is the Python early key/count check the Gate Runner runs. G3S and G3K must PASS before G3 locks the design.
- Stage 5 reuses the existing bots: the Lead Designer writes the interpretation, the Second Designer checks the inference, and the Risk Reviewer audits every number (and may recompute arithmetic from sealed results, but never sees the answer key). There are no dedicated finish bots.
- Stage 4 order: R-A, R-B, and SQL build independently on the frozen, hashed snapshot (not live systems) → freeze each → fixtures (G4a) → Gate Runner reconciles R-A vs R-B vs SQL (G4b) → Risk Reviewer checks the reconciled evidence (G4c) → release gate (G4d). SQL source gates already passed in Stage 3.
- A difference in reconciliation is traced to a cause and repaired from the design. The design itself does not change without owner change control and a new version.
- Large artifacts are requested in parts from the start and joined with an assembly script that logs every transform.
- After the freeze, the earlier key preparer from the ChatGPT (Version 1) side holds the private key and scores both versions blind. Live roles never see the key.

## Workflow changes (owner-approved 2026-11-22)

These fit inside the existing roles and gates; no stage, role or send is added. Standing rules 37–44.

**Four known fixes (gate code).**
1. **Attempt ids.** Every gate run has an attempt id and its outcome is recorded, TOOL_ERROR included (`artifacts/gates/attempts/<id>.json`). A TOOL_ERROR also becomes the current record, so an older PASS never stays current after a failed retry. The Coordinator requests each gate run with `--attempt=<id>` (the Gate Runner runs it) and advances only when `python3 -m gates.attempt <GATE> <root> <id>` exits 0 for that same attempt.
2. **Snapshot binding.** The G3S manifest names the frozen snapshot by `id` and `sha256`, the Database Connector reports the identity of the snapshot it actually opened for every query, and G3S fails unless the two match (repair round 1); the G3S record keeps them. `artifacts/stage4/outputs.json` names the snapshot Stage 4 ran on (`snapshot` {id, sha256, path}); G4a and G4b fail unless it is the exact snapshot the current G3S PASS checked. Switching from snapshot A to snapshot B needs a fresh G3S run on B.
3. **Precheck by record, not by file name.** A Stage 5 review packet carries the runner-written G5P record (`artifacts/gates/history/G5P-*.json`) as an input; G0 checks it is a PASS for the exact claims ledger listed in the same packet (same path and sha256) and that nothing G5P read has changed, including every report and companion it reviewed (repair round 1).
4. **One finish order everywhere:** owner approves the exact finish package -> R `complete finish` -> G5 -> R `finalize` -> G6. G5 and G6 check this order from the recorded times (repair round 2).

**Stage 1 (decision).** The decision document's options always include keeping the current policy and waiting or deferring the decision (with what would end the wait), beside the actions under consideration. The agreed question and the decision rule are worded so that they do not presuppose the conclusion anyone hopes for; analysts get the decision rule, not the hoped-for answer. The Second Designer's blind rebuild and the Risk Reviewer's red team check both points.

**Stage 2 (framing).** Two questions are added to the existing reviews:
- Risk Reviewer: "Could we observe the finding this question measures even if the proposed action would do little good? What evidence would distinguish that situation?" Start from the actual decision and the proposed question; give one plausible alternative explanation if it changes how the executive could use the answer; check whether the brief and captures already address it; then recommend keeping the question, clarifying its purpose, or a revision for the owner. A descriptive question can be entirely adequate when the owner uses it to trigger an investigation or a trial.
- Second Designer: "Could two competent analysts read a key term differently and produce answers that would matter differently to this executive? Give one example where the meanings come apart." Name the term, spell out the two readings in plain words, and build one small hypothetical example that separates them. If the record settles the meaning, cite it and recommend the exact wording; if both readings remain plausible and the choice matters, send one focused question through the parent; if the wording is clear or both readings serve the decision equally, let the framing stand.
The Lead Designer folds in resolved wording; the parent routes any consequential open question to the owner and the Gate Runner captures the answer; the owner still locks the exact framing.

**Stage 3 (design).** The design carries small hand-checkable fixtures where a plausible mistake would flip the decision: a value exactly at the threshold versus just over it (for example exactly 20% versus over 20%), pooled versus per-unit results, missing values, and duplicate join rows. Each is declared in `decision_flip_fixtures` with the fixture id and the decision it would flip, or `not_applicable` with a reason (G3).

**Stage 4 (execution).** Agreement among R-A, R-B and SQL is not enough: all three are also checked against the known answers of the locked fixtures, including the decision-flip fixtures (G4a, before G4b). A tolerance is allowed only on fields where rounding legitimately differs, declared at the design lock with a `rounding_reason` (G3); keys, identifiers, counts, decision fields and every other field match exactly. Tolerances are never widened after results are seen (control C39).

**Stage 5 (interpretation).** The write-up asks "Which action is best justified, given uncertainty, costs, reversibility and alternatives?" and treats no change as a real option alongside investigation, pilot and adoption. It states separately what causal claim, if any, the design supports.

**Across stages.**
- **Checkable reviewer tasks.** Each review packet asks for something the reviewer can check: recompute a named number, open a named source, run a hidden fixture, or name a specific unsupported inference. "Looks fine" is not a review (docs/judgment_checks.md).
- **Keep the first independent answer.** A reviewer or builder submits its first answer, and the Gate Runner captures and seals it, before the author sees a peer's conclusion. A revision is a new version; both are kept, so a later check can tell a correction from a case where review displaced a correct answer.
- **Three claims, judged separately.** Does the result reproduce on the frozen data? Does it hold up under other defensible analytical choices? Does the recommendation fit the action the business faces? Each needs its own evidence in the claims ledger; the Risk Reviewer leads on the first, the Second Designer on the second and third.
- **Light review-cost log.** The parent appends one line per review round to `artifacts/review_costs.jsonl`: stage, role, round, must-fix and advisory counts, findings later judged false alarms, revisions that made a correct item wrong, and elapsed minutes. It is a record for later comparison, not a gate.
