# The five stages, explained

1. **Start.** Find the decision behind the request and who makes it, by talking to the person asking. A Stakeholder bot plays that business person in a group chat with the owner and the parent: it opens the case, answers the parent's clarifying questions from the brief (saying when the brief is silent and calling a preference a preference), and the owner weighs in or adds a twist. Every message relied on is captured word for word. The Lead Designer writes the Stage 1 decision document (the agreed decision and question) from the brief and those captures; the Second Designer rebuilds it blind and the Risk Reviewer red-teams it. The owner locks it in his own words; the Stakeholder's approval never counts as a lock. The parent pins the workflow and checks it at the start gate G1.
2. **Framing.** Turn it into one precise question: who or what is measured, over which window, what counts as better, and what threshold changes the decision. The framing goes back to the group chat: the Stakeholder reacts as a business reader, the owner weighs in, and the owner locks the exact framing in his own words. The framing cites the brief and the captured chat. The Second Designer critiques the framing; the Risk Reviewer checks it alongside that critique, and later reviews G2F feasibility.
3. **Feasibility, then Design.** First check whether the data can answer the question (yes, partly, or no), looking only at structure and quality, not values or the answer key. Then define every metric exactly, handle the edge cases, and write test cases with known answers. The Second Designer writes its own independent design from the same Stage 3 inputs, without seeing the lead's draft or reasoning, then compares the two and reports differences. The Risk Reviewer red-teams the design. The SQL Analyst writes SELECT-only source gates (extraction completeness, keys, joins, counts) against the design; the parent runs them; the Risk Reviewer reviews them. Lock the design with the owner.
4. **Execution.** Build the analysis twice in R and once in SQL, each independently, on the frozen hashed snapshot (not live systems). Test on the fixtures, have the Gate Runner reconcile SQL against R (G4b), have the Risk Reviewer check the reconciled evidence, and release only when every gate passes.
5. **Finish.** Interpret only the sealed results, have the claims checked, recommend an action, and score blind afterwards. Stage 5 reuses the existing bots: the Lead Designer writes the interpretation, the Second Designer checks the inference, and the Risk Reviewer audits every number. The Risk Reviewer may recompute arithmetic from the sealed results, but never sees the answer key.

A mistake early carries forward, so Stage 3 matters most.
